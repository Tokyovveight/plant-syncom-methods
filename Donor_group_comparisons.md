# Methods for identifying candidate taxa by comparing donor groups

These methods estimate different quantities. Agreement among analyses of the same abundance matrix is not independent biological replication.

| Approach | Data and question | Use for candidate nomination | Main limitation | Validation needed | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Group tests | Differences in measured abundance or transformed representation between groups. | Screen simple predefined contrasts. | Confounding, compositionality and multiple testing remain. | Confirm in a covariate-aware design and plant experiment. | Nearing et al., 2022 |
| DESeq2 / edgeR | Negative-binomial models of sequencing counts with library normalization. | Model count contrasts and covariates. | Normalization assumptions can fail with widespread microbiome shifts; RNA-seq origins do not ensure microbiome calibration. | Check normalization sensitivity and validate candidates. | Love et al., 2014; Robinson et al., 2010 |
| metagenomeSeq | Normalized counts with models addressing zero-rich microbiome measurements. | Screen sparse count-based contrasts. | Zero modelling and normalization do not eliminate compositional bias. | Check sensitivity to filtering and alternative models. | Paulson et al., 2013 |
| ALDEx2 | Monte Carlo uncertainty with log-ratio contrasts. | Rank taxa differing in relative representation. | Interpretation depends on denominator and zero treatment; no absolute-abundance claim. | Confirm with independent or quantitative measurements. | Fernandes et al., 2014 |
| ANCOM-BC2 | Model-based log-absolute-abundance contrasts under bias-correction assumptions; multigroup, covariate and supported repeated-measures designs. | Nominate taxa associated with a specified contrast. | Assumptions and structural-zero handling matter; correction is not direct absolute quantification. | Match design to model and validate selected taxa. | Lin and Peddada, 2024 |
| MaAsLin2 | Multivariable associations under specified normalization, transformations and model. | Adjust candidate associations for measured covariates. | Unmeasured confounding and chosen preprocessing remain influential. | Use appropriate subject effects and independent validation. | Mallick et al., 2021 |
| LinDA | Model-based log-absolute-abundance contrasts estimated from compositions through bias-corrected linear models. | Estimate covariate-adjusted taxon contrasts. | Bias correction assumes the coefficient mode corresponds to null effects; dense nonzero effects can violate this. No direct absolute quantification. | Check robustness and test recovered representatives. | Zhou et al., 2022b |
| Songbird | Differential rankings from a multinomial regression model. | Rank taxa for follow-up log-ratio contrasts associated with metadata. | Ranks are relative; they are not individual absolute effect sizes or significance tests. | Validate prediction and biologically meaningful ratios. | Morton et al., 2019 |
| LEfSe | Group discrimination with rank tests and linear discriminant effect ranking. | Explore taxa distinguishing predefined classes. | Discrimination does not establish causation; correlated design and confounding require care. | Confirm with design-aware analysis and intervention. | Segata et al., 2011 |
| Indicator analysis | Specificity and fidelity to a group or group combination. | Nominate taxa characteristic of a donor state. | An indicator can reflect the environment without providing a useful plant function. | Replicate association and test function in a recipient. | Dufrêne and Legendre, 1997; De Cáceres and Legendre, 2009 |
| Niche breadth | Levins-type evenness of proportional occurrence or abundance across defined habitats or resource states; availability-adjusted variants and a specified null model. | Distinguish broad occupancy from context-associated taxa. | Index, habitat availability and randomization define the result; breadth is not universal stress tolerance. | Test across matching and contrasting recipient contexts. | Levins, 1968; Hurlbert, 1978; Pandit et al., 2009; Isabwe et al., 2022 |
| Supervised prediction | Phenotype or state prediction using microbial features and covariates. | Prioritize features or combinations associated with an outcome. | Leakage, correlated features and confounding can inflate importance. | Validate on held-out independent groups and test candidates. | Topçuoğlu et al., 2020; Emmenegger et al., 2023 |
